A practical checklist for newcomers

How to read a German official letter: five things to check first

A letter from a German authority can be hard to understand even when you know the language. Before drafting a reply, separate the facts you can verify from the parts that need professional advice.

Important: This guide offers general information, not legal, tax, immigration or social-benefits advice. If the letter concerns an appeal, a threatened loss of benefits, a court matter or an urgent deadline, contact a qualified professional or recognised advice centre promptly. Do not rely on an AI summary to calculate a legal deadline.

1. Identify the sender and the reference number

Look for the authority's name, contact details and Aktenzeichen (file reference). A Jobcenter, Familienkasse, Ausländerbehörde and health insurer handle different matters. Verify unfamiliar contact details through the authority's official website rather than a link or phone number in a suspicious message.

2. Find the action requested

Is the letter a decision (Bescheid), a request for documents (Nachweise), a hearing (Anhörung) or an appointment (Termin)? Highlight the exact sentence that says what the sender wants. The rest may be background or an explanation.

3. Mark every date, but do not guess the deadline

Note the letter date, any date of receipt, appointment date and stated response period separately. Keep the envelope and a copy of the letter. Which date legally starts a period can depend on the procedure and how the letter was delivered. If that matters, ask the authority or a qualified adviser in time.

4. Make a short list of requested documents

Write down each document, amount, period and form the letter mentions. Check whether the sender offers an official portal or another specified contact route. Keep copies of what you submit and a record of when you submitted it.

5. Check the consequences and your options

Read the section explaining what happens if you do not respond, and any Rechtsbehelfsbelehrung (information about remedies). Do not assume that every letter can be challenged in the same way or within the same period. For a decision you disagree with, seek individual advice instead of copying a generic objection template.

A five-minute checklist

  1. Keep the original letter and envelope safe.
  2. Record the sender, reference number and all relevant dates.
  3. Write one sentence describing the action requested.
  4. List the documents or information you may need.
  5. Seek qualified help early if the deadline or consequences are unclear.
