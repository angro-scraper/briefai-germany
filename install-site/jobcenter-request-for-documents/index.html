Jobcenter asks for documents: what should you check?

A letter headed “Aufforderung zur Mitwirkung” may ask you to provide information or evidence. This checklist helps you read the request in your own letter before you respond.

Important: This is general information, not legal or social-benefits advice. Your actual letter and the instructions from your responsible Jobcenter control what is requested and when. If benefits may be affected, seek qualified individual advice promptly.

Five things to mark in the original

  1. Sender and reference: Identify the responsible Jobcenter, letter date, case reference (Aktenzeichen) and how to contact it. Keep the full original.
  2. Exactly what is requested: List each document or answer separately, including the period it covers. The Federal Employment Agency says the evidence required depends on the application and individual situation; do not assume a generic list is complete.
  3. The date in your letter: Copy the stated submission date or deadline into your calendar. This page deliberately does not give a universal response period. If a document is unavailable, ask the Jobcenter early what to do next.
  4. Submission route: Follow the route specified in the letter or confirm the accepted route with your Jobcenter. The official Jobcenter online service can be used to send messages and evidence. Keep copies and a submission confirmation where available.
  5. Notice about possible consequences: Read any warning about non-cooperation in full. Under § 66 SGB I, withholding or withdrawing benefits for missing cooperation requires, among other conditions, a written warning and an appropriate period set for compliance. Whether that applies to your situation needs an individual assessment.

Do not send an original document by default

For evidence accompanying an application, the Federal Employment Agency explicitly says not to submit originals: documents handed in are digitised. If your particular letter asks to inspect an original in person, or its instructions are unclear, confirm the requirement with your Jobcenter. Make sure any copy you submit is legible and covers the requested dates.

A request for documents is not automatically a final decision on benefits. Read the heading and all pages: a formal decision (Bescheid) and a request for information can have different consequences. If you cannot tell which you received, ask the Jobcenter or an independent adviser.
