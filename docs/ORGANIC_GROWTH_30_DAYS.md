# BriefAI Germany — 30-day organic growth operating plan

## Objective and guardrails

Target: 1,000 new registered Firebase Authentication accounts and 20 paid
subscriptions in 30 days without paid ads. This is a stretch target, not a
guaranteed result. Work must focus on useful, accurate help for people who
receive German administrative letters.

Every public message must:

- identify AMIA Technologies / BriefAI when mentioning the product;
- say that BriefAI is an information tool and not legal advice when the topic
  involves an individual administrative or legal issue;
- avoid testimonials, claims of results, urgency, or fabricated user stories;
- follow the destination's posting rules; and
- be approved immediately before it is posted or sent.

Store links:

- iOS: <https://apps.apple.com/app/id6757674090>
- Android: <https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.briefai.briefai_germany>

## Source of truth for measurement

| Metric | Source | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registered accounts | Firebase Authentication user count | The only authoritative account count; includes email, Google and Apple. |
| Activation | First completed analysis | Separates curiosity from product value. |
| Paid conversion | Verified Apple/Google purchase status | Subscription outcome, not a store click. |
| Channel attribution | Channel-specific landing/store link plus first-party consented analytics | Shows which channel should receive more effort. |
| Retention | 1-day and 7-day active signed-in users | Reveals whether the promise matches the product. |

The current `Registrations` funnel card must **not** be used as the account
total: it is consent-based analytics and does not presently represent every
Apple/Google registration. The `Users` metric should instead be the Firebase
Authentication count. Fix the admin Google sign-in before the first weekly
review, otherwise the baseline is unknown.

Decision rules:

- By day 7, retain only channels that produced meaningful clicks, sign-ups or
  high-quality conversations; stop repetitive posts that only produce reach.
- By day 14, publish only the two best language/channel combinations twice per
  week; use the remaining capacity for partner outreach and search content.
- By day 21, if activation is weak, improve the first-analysis flow before
  increasing distribution.
- By day 30, compare accounts, activation and paid conversion by channel and
  keep the highest-intent two channels for the next month.

## Channel priorities

1. **Short, useful video:** TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.
   Publish one original vertical video on the same real-world topic across all
   three channels, with native captions in the audience language. Do not make
   medical/legal determinations in the video.
2. **Diaspora communities:** Facebook and relevant moderated communities.
   Lead with the answer to a concrete question; mention BriefAI only where
   rules allow it and the mention is relevant.
3. **Search content:** A focused library of factual guides such as “What to
   check in a Jobcenter letter” and “How to find a deadline in a German
   authority letter.” Each page needs sources, a clear author/company identity,
   a legal-information disclaimer, and one relevant app CTA. Do not create
   mass-generated keyword pages.
4. **App-store conversion:** Keep German and English screenshots focused on a
   visible before/after outcome: letter → plain-language explanation → deadline
   → reply draft. Test one store-listing variable at a time.
5. **Partners and directories:** Reach out individually to trusted diaspora
   newsletters, language schools, migrant advice organisations, translators,
   and app directories. Offer a transparent demo link and request feedback,
   never reviews in exchange for compensation.
6. **Maker credibility:** Prepare one Product Hunt launch after the public
   login/purchase experience is stable. Use a founder's personal account and
   ask for feedback, never votes. Product Hunt is a credibility channel, not
   the main source of German diaspora users.

## Weekly execution

### Week 1 — Measurement and conversion foundation

- Repair admin access and take a verified baseline of Firebase Auth users,
  installs and purchases.
- Define one reusable visual template and six captions (German, Serbian/Croatian,
  Romanian, Bulgarian, Turkish and Ukrainian/Russian only after native-language
  review).
- Produce three short demos: Jobcenter deadline, Ausländerbehörde request,
  and Krankenkasse/Finanzamt letter. Use fictionalised sample text only.
- Audit App Store and Play listings: product promise, first three screenshots,
  price copy, privacy language, and store links.
- Create channel-specific links and a measurement sheet before posting.

### Week 2 — Helpful content and community discovery

- Publish three useful vertical videos and one longer explanatory post.
- Publish two sourced guides on the website; make each answer a real question
  before mentioning the app.
- Identify ten community/partner candidates, read their rules, and prepare a
  separate post or outreach message for each allowed destination.
- Submit a draft to Product Hunt only if the login, trial/credits and purchases
  have passed a real-device test.

### Week 3 — Repeat proven messages

- Compare source links and account/activation data; use the top two topics in
  two additional languages.
- Answer up to five concrete community questions with a helpful response and
  transparent BriefAI attribution when that group permits it.
- Contact five relevant administrators/partners one-to-one with a short,
  personalised request for permission to share a useful guide. No bulk DMs.
- Invite early users to report friction via the support channel; do not request
  positive reviews.

### Week 4 — Conversion and retention

- Test one clear store-listing improvement at a time: first screenshot, title
  subtitle, or short preview. Do not change all variables at once.
- Turn the best guide into a video, carousel and FAQ, preserving source links
  and disclaimer.
- Prepare a re-engagement message for users who explicitly opted in to product
  notifications; it must be about an actual product improvement, not pressure.
- Produce a month-end report: accounts, activation, subscription conversion,
  top source, top language, failed channels, next-month hypothesis.

## Content pillars and safe hooks

| Pillar | Useful hook | CTA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadlines | “Where is the deadline in this German letter?” | “See the letter in clear language.” |
| Official letters | “Four details to identify before you respond.” | “BriefAI can organise the key details; it is not legal advice.” |
| Language barrier | “You do not need to guess what this paragraph means.” | “Read an explanation in your chosen language.” |
| Reply draft | “How to prepare a formal German reply draft for review.” | “Create a draft, then review it before sending.” |
| Organisation | “Keep important dates and documents together.” | “Try the first five analyses free.” |

## Required content assets

- Three 20–35 second vertical videos with captions and a clear end card.
- Six language-specific captions reviewed by a fluent speaker before publishing.
- One factual help page per validated high-intent question.
- One 3-screen store screenshot set for each leading language.
- One founder profile and transparent company description for directories.
- A single support contact and a concise privacy/legal-information notice.

## What not to do

- Do not claim that BriefAI replaces a lawyer, government office, adviser or
  translator.
- Do not comment as though AMIA were an unrelated satisfied user.
- Do not post the same promotional text across groups or send bulk messages.
- Do not buy reviews, installs, followers, upvotes or backlinks.
- Do not optimise around install count alone; paid conversion begins with a
  working first analysis and reliable sign-in/purchase flow.
